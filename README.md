# Bayesian Robotics Book — Companion Notebooks

Computational examples for **Bayesian Methods for Autonomous Robotics and Intelligent Infrastructure Systems: Probabilistic Perception, Estimation, and Digital Twins**, by **Khalid M. Mosalam**, Taisei Professor of Civil Engineering, University of California, Berkeley.

## Contributors and maintainers

Other contributors to the book are acknowledged in the book.

## Notebooks

Notebooks are named by topic.

| Topic | Notebook | Chapters supported | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bayesian inference basics | [bayesian-inference-basics.ipynb](notebooks/bayesian-inference-basics.ipynb) | 2 — Bayes' Theorem: Foundation of Probabilistic Inference; 3 — Bayesian Updating: Recursive Belief Refinement; 4 — Bayesian Inference: Estimating Hidden States; 5 — Bayesian Prediction: Forecasting Future States | Discrete robot localization, stop-sign detection, radar-based tracking, pedestrian motion forecasting, and Gaussian process prediction |
| Sensor models | [sensor-models.ipynb](notebooks/sensor-models.ipynb) | 7 — Sensor Models | Range and bearing geometry, measurement likelihood, Gauss–Newton position estimate, local observability, and pinhole camera projection |
| Kalman filters | [kalman-filters.ipynb](notebooks/kalman-filters.ipynb) | 8 — Kalman Filter: Bayesian Estimation Over Time; 9 — Extended Kalman Filter (EKF); 10 — Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) | One-dimensional Kalman filter, EKF, and UKF examples, and the scalar Kalman gain |
| Particle filters | [particle-filters.ipynb](notebooks/particle-filters.ipynb) | 11 — Particle Filters | One-dimensional particle-filter localization, a step-by-step small example, and a comparison with the EKF |
| Monte Carlo methods | [monte-carlo-methods.ipynb](notebooks/monte-carlo-methods.ipynb) | 15 — Monte Carlo Methods | Expected cost of robot paths and estimation of π |
| Motion planning under uncertainty | [motion-planning-under-uncertainty.ipynb](notebooks/motion-planning-under-uncertainty.ipynb) | 22 — Motion Planning Under Uncertainty | Belief geometry, risk-sensitive path choice, and information-gathering actions |

All six notebooks run independently from top to bottom. Their numerical/plotting imports use only **NumPy** and **Matplotlib**. Random simulations have fixed seeds. All observations and costs are synthetic or illustrative; the small particle-filter walkthrough uses the book's fixed positions and resampling indices. No external dataset or account is required.

## Setup and use

Requirements: Python 3.12, NumPy and Matplotlib (versions pinned in `requirements.txt`), and JupyterLab to run the notebooks.

Create a Python 3.12 virtual environment outside the repository. From the repository root:

```bash
python3.12 -m venv ../robotics-book-venv
source ../robotics-book-venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt jupyterlab
cd notebooks
jupyter lab
```

Open a notebook and run all cells from top to bottom. Starting JupyterLab in `notebooks/` lets the figure exports use `../figures/`.

## Figures and expected results

The [figures/](figures/) directory contains six PDFs generated by the sensor-models and motion-planning notebooks:

- `sensor-models-geometry.pdf`: range and bearing geometry.
- `sensor-models-likelihood.pdf`: joint measurement likelihood.
- `sensor-models-gauss-newton.pdf`: the Gauss–Newton position estimate and iterations.
- `sensor-models-camera.pdf`: ideal and noisy camera projections.
- `motion-planning-risk-tradeoff.pdf`: risk-sensitive path choice with mean costs 10.0 and 10.8.
- `motion-planning-information-tradeoff.pdf`: final covariance trace versus distance from the belief mean to the goal.

The sensor estimate converges to approximately **(5.245, 3.088)**. The particle-filter walkthrough reproduces all three book tables and the means **7.0631** before resampling and **7.0608** after the fixed draw. Motion-planning goal-distance labels refer explicitly to the **belief mean**.
